## Findings

**F9 — Stage 1 §10 "It must accept an unchanged export produced by this service" and "Test control calls have a 10-second timeout"; stage 4
"retaining … snapshots" (L10/L12).** Tokens that survive an import (L12) still read the state they were minted on, and the export writes each of them as a
**fully rendered frozen result**. One rendered result per distinct window means the export grows as (number of reads) × (window size). Sequence: state A,
300 payments, then 1000 rounds of (one payment, one first-page statement), so 1000 distinct windows of 301 to 1300 entries; then import an unrelated state B
**without reset**, then export:
- `GET /_test/export` → **378.9 MB in 13.2 s** (over the 10 s test-control limit); container memory 25 → 302 MiB during the export;
- `POST /_test/import` of that unchanged export → refused (over the 64 MiB cap, connection closed).
Repro: `python3 reviews/stage4/tools/frozen_export_growth.py http://127.0.0.1:<port> 1000`.
Direction (owner's choice): export each retained old state **once** (its ledger records, or just the payments and revisions the surviving recipes can
read) and keep the tokens as recipes pointing at it. The export then grows with the retained states, not with reads × window, and the digest dedupe becomes unnecessary.
